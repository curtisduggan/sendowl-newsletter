🔍 The Deep Dive
You already have a product. You just call it “work.”

Jeff Gargas has started more than ten businesses. The one that stuck was Teach Better, an organization that helps educators improve their craft. Along the way, he noticed something: the teachers he worked with had deep, specific expertise, but almost none of them thought of it as something they could sell.

So he built the EDUpreneur Mastermind. It meets every Thursday at 7 PM EST, costs $79 a month, and the premise is simple: if you've spent years building something that works in a classroom, someone will pay you to teach them how to do it too.

The results have been concrete. Three members went from earning less than $50,000 to six figures. One is approaching $500,000. Another is on track for $750,000 this year. They sell curriculum guides, books, online courses, coaching packages, and speaking engagements.

What they don't sell is motivation. Jeff is clear about who shouldn't join: anyone already burnt out, anyone who's only in it for money, and anyone who keeps saying “I don't have time” before they've tried. The people who succeed are the ones who already have the expertise and just need the business skills and the community to figure out how to package it.

The pattern Jeff sees over and over: the expertise already exists. The gap is always confidence and distribution, never knowledge.

Andrea Bitner is a good example. She joined the mastermind describing herself as “just a teacher.” Within months she had built enough confidence in her pricing and positioning to book speaking engagements and sell digital resources she'd been giving away for free.

This isn't just a teacher story. It's a creator story. If you've built something that works, whether it's a classroom system, a design process, a fitness methodology, or a recipe collection, you already have a product. The question is whether you'll package it.

⚡ Quick Wins

Price for what it took to learn, not what it takes to download.

A PDF that contains five years of teaching experience isn't worth $5 just because it's a PDF. Price it based on the outcome it delivers and the expertise behind it.

Start with your smallest viable audience.

Ann Kullberg launched a digital magazine with 200 fan mail addresses and got 50 subscriptions in two weeks. You don't need 10,000 followers. You need 200 people who care.

Automate delivery before your third sale.

Manual fulfillment works for your first two customers. By the third, you're losing time you should spend creating. Set up automated delivery before it becomes a bottleneck.
