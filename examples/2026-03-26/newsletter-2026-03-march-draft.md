## 🔍 The Deep Dive

### Your niche doesn't need to be big. It needs to be yours.

Ann Kullberg has been selling colored pencil art instruction for 27 years. She started with a $2.50 monthly magazine subscription and 200 people from her fan mail list. Fifty checks showed up in two weeks. Today she has over 65 book titles, a monthly magazine, online classes, and a team of five. She works about eight hours a week. She's 70.

Colored pencil art is not a big market. Ann didn't care. She knew the audience intimately because she was one of them. She heard what they wanted because she was teaching workshops in 44 states. And when she saw an opportunity, she moved on it: "I can wake up in the morning and decide we're going to have a free shipping Friday, and in an hour we've got a free shipping Friday."

Compare that to Dupé Aleru's story. Dupé spent four years building a following of over 450,000 people with motivational content. The engagement was strong. The audience was loyal. But she couldn't build a business on it.

Her diagnosis was blunt: "Motivation doesn't convert. It just builds a loyal following."

So she pivoted to digital products and automations coaching. She sells Notion templates, Google Sheets tools, and coaching packages. She says your sub-niche needs to fall within one of five main content pillars (finance, relationships, beauty, travel, or food) or you're fighting an uphill battle. Digital marketing falls in the money and finance pillar. That's why it converts.

**The pattern across both stories is the same.** Ann found a tiny niche and owned it for three decades. Dupé built a massive audience in the wrong niche and had to start over in the right one. The size of the audience didn't matter. The fit between the niche and the business model did.

If you're sitting on a skill that feels too narrow to sell, that narrowness might be exactly what makes it durable. Nobody is competing with Ann Kullberg for colored pencil portrait instruction. And nobody with 450K motivational followers is automatically going to outsell Dupé's Notion templates.

The niche is never the limitation. It's the advantage.

## ⭐ Seller Spotlight

### Ann Kullberg

Ann Kullberg works eight hours a week. She's 70 years old and runs a digital publishing business from her home.

Twenty-seven years ago, the picture was different. She was a divorced single mother with a severely handicapped son, drawing colored pencil portraits that took 120 hours each. She needed to finish two a month to cover the bills. That meant 60-hour work weeks for eleven years.

A publisher spotted her portrait work and asked her to write a book. She included her website address in the bio. People came to her. She launched a $2.50/month digital magazine. Fifty checks arrived in two weeks from her fan mail list of 200 people.

From there: tutorial kits that "flew off the shelves," a bad business partnership that pushed her into publishing other artists' books (65+ titles now), a pivot from live workshops to online classes during Covid, and a team of five part-time employees spread across three states and two countries who keep everything running.

Her philosophy: "I think the main ingredient for success is hanging in there. Just not quitting."

She found [SendOwl](https://www.sendowl.com) around 2012 to automate digital delivery and has never looked elsewhere. "I'm so happy with the service that I've never even looked for an alternative."

## ⚡ Quick Wins

**Your first 200 fans are your business.** Ann Kullberg launched a magazine with 200 fan mail addresses and had paying subscribers within two weeks. You don't need 10K followers to start selling. You need a small group of people who already trust you, and something specific to offer them.

**Name your niche in your product title.** "Colored pencil portrait techniques" outsells "art tutorial" every time. Dupé Aleru's framework: your sub-niche needs to sit inside a monetizable pillar. If your product title could describe anyone's product, it's not specific enough.

**Automate delivery before it breaks you.** Ann manually emailed UK customers their download links for years before finding SendOwl. If you're still sending files by hand, fix it now. The time you're spending on manual delivery is time you're not spending on making the next product.
